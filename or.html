Module 1: Linear Programming Problem

Q1. LPP Formulation (Milk Plant)
Question: A milk plant manufactures two types of products A and B and sells them at a profit of Rs. 5 on type A and Rs. 3 on type B. Each product is processed on two machines G and H. Type A requires 1 min on G and 2 mins on H; type B requires 1 min on G and 1 min on H. Machine G is available for 6 hours 40 minutes, while machine H is available for 8 hours 20 minutes. Formulate the LPP for maximizing the profit.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Identify Decision Variables: Let x₁ be the number of units of Product A and x₂ be the number of units of Product B.
  • Formulate Objective Function: The profit is Rs 5 on A and Rs 3 on B. Goal is to Maximize Z = 5x₁ + 3x₂.
  • Convert Time:
    Machine G = 6 hours 40 mins = (6 × 60) + 40 = 400 minutes.
    Machine H = 8 hours 20 mins = (8 × 60) + 20 = 500 minutes.
  • Formulate Constraints:
    For Machine G: 1x₁ + 1x₂ ≤ 400
    For Machine H: 2x₁ + 1x₂ ≤ 500
  • Non-negativity: x₁, x₂ ≥ 0
Final Answer:
Maximize Z = 5x₁ + 3x₂
Subject to: x₁ + x₂ ≤ 400, 2x₁ + x₂ ≤ 500, x₁, x₂ ≥ 0
Q2. LPP Formulation (Animal Feed)
Question: An animal feed company must produce 200 kg of a mixture consisting of ingredients A and B daily. A costs Rs. 3 per kg and B costs Rs. 8 per kg. Not more than 80 kg of A can be used and at least 60 kg of B must be used. Formulate the LPP to minimize cost.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Identify Decision Variables: Let x₁ = kg of ingredient A, x₂ = kg of ingredient B.
  • Objective Function: Minimize Cost Z = 3x₁ + 8x₂.
  • Constraints:
    Total mixture required: x₁ + x₂ = 200
    Maximum limit for A: x₁ ≤ 80
    Minimum limit for B: x₂ ≥ 60
Final Answer:
Minimize Z = 3x₁ + 8x₂
Subject to: x₁ + x₂ = 200, x₁ ≤ 80, x₂ ≥ 60, x₁, x₂ ≥ 0
Q3. LPP Formulation (Software Company)
Question: Profit from web app is 60,000, mobile app is 80,000. Web app requires 100 hrs programming, 20 hrs testing. Mobile app requires 120 hrs programming, 40 hrs testing. Available: 1200 programming hrs, 320 testing hrs. At least 4 mobile apps must be developed. Formulate LPP.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Decision Variables: Let x₁ = Web applications, x₂ = Mobile applications.
  • Objective Function: Maximize Profit Z = 60000x₁ + 80000x₂.
  • Constraints:
    Programming limits: 100x₁ + 120x₂ ≤ 1200
    Testing limits: 20x₁ + 40x₂ ≤ 320
    Minimum mobile apps: x₂ ≥ 4
Q4. Graphical Method (Maximization)
Question: Solve by graphical method: Maximize Z = 8x + y. Subject to: x + y ≤ 40, 2x + y ≤ 60, x, y ≥ 0.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Find coordinates for Line 1 (x + y = 40): Put x=0 → y=40 (0,40). Put y=0 → x=40 (40,0).
  • Find coordinates for Line 2 (2x + y = 60): Put x=0 → y=60 (0,60). Put y=0 → x=30 (30,0).
  • Find Intersection Point: Subtracting Line 1 from Line 2 gives x=20. Putting x=20 in Line 1 gives y=20. Intersection = (20, 20).
  • Evaluate Corner Points: The feasible region points are (0,0), (30,0), (20,20), and (0,40).

Answer: The maximum value of Z is 240 at x=30, y=0.

Q5. Graphical Method (Minimization)
Question: Solve by graphical method: Minimize Z = 5x + 7y. Subject to: x + y ≥ 6, x, y ≥ 0.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • Find coordinates for Line (x + y = 6): Put x=0 → y=6 (0,6). Put y=0 → x=6 (6,0).
  • Identify Feasible Region: Since it is "≥", the region is unbounded away from origin. The corner points are (6,0) and (0,6).
  • Evaluate Corner Points:
    At (6,0): Z = 5(6) + 7(0) = 30
    At (0,6): Z = 5(0) + 7(6) = 42

Answer: The minimum value of Z is 30 at x=6, y=0.

Q6. Dual Formulation
Question: Formulate the dual of: Minimize Z = 6x + 5y. Subject to: x + y ≥ 8, 2x + 3y ≥ 18, 3x + y ≥ 15, x, y ≥ 0.

Step-by-Step Solution:

For a Minimization Primal, the Dual is Maximization. The RHS constants become objective coefficients. The objective coefficients become RHS limits. Rows become columns.

Let dual variables be w₁, w₂, w₃.

Dual Problem:
Maximize W = 8w₁ + 18w₂ + 15w₃
Subject to:
1w₁ + 2w₂ + 3w₃ ≤ 6
1w₁ + 3w₂ + 1w₃ ≤ 5
w₁, w₂, w₃ ≥ 0

Module 2: Transportation Problem

Q1. North-West Corner (NWC) Method
Question: Solve using NWC Method. Supplies: 20, 30, 25. Demands: 10, 35, 30. Find cost and check degeneracy.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Allocate starting from top-left.
1. (S1, D1): Demand 10, Supply 20 → Allocate 10.
2. (S1, D2): Remaining Supply 10, Demand 35 → Allocate 10.
3. (S2, D2): Remaining Demand 25, Supply 30 → Allocate 25.
4. (S2, D3): Remaining Supply 5, Demand 30 → Allocate 5.
5. (S3, D3): Remaining Demand 25, Supply 25 → Allocate 25.

S/DD₁D₂D₃Supply
S₁10 (Cost:4)10 (Cost:6)(Cost:8)20
S₂(Cost:3)25 (Cost:5)5 (Cost:7)30
S₃(Cost:4)(Cost:6)25 (Cost:5)25
Demand10353075

Step 2: Total Cost = (10×4) + (10×6) + (25×5) + (5×7) + (25×5) = 385.

Step 3: Degeneracy Check = No. of allocations is 5. m+n-1 = 3+3-1 = 5. Since 5 = 5, solution is Non-Degenerate.

Q2. Least Cost Method (LCM)
Question: Solve using LCM. Supplies: 20, 30, 40. Demands: 30, 20, 40. Find cost and check degeneracy.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Always pick the lowest cost cell in the entire matrix.
1. Lowest is 5 at (S3, D2). Allocate min(40, 20) = 20. D2 fulfilled.
2. Next lowest is 8 at (S1, D1) and (S3, D3). Pick (S1, D1). Allocate min(20, 30) = 20. S1 fulfilled.
3. Next lowest is 8 at (S3, D3). Allocate min(20, 40) = 20.
4. Remaining demands mapped to S2.

S/DD₁D₂D₃Supply
S₁20 (C:8)(C:6)(C:10)20
S₂10 (C:12)(C:7)20 (C:9)30
S₃(C:11)20 (C:5)20 (C:8)40

Total Cost = (20×8) + (10×12) + (20×9) + (20×5) + (20×8) = 720.

Q3. Vogel's Approximation Method (VAM)
Question: Solve using VAM. Supplies: 25, 35, 30. Demands: 20, 30, 40. Find cost and check degeneracy.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Calculate Penalties (Difference of 2 lowest costs in rows/cols).
Max penalty is in Row 1. Minimum cost is 3 at (S1, D2). Allocate 25.
Repeat penalties for remaining rows/cols until all supply/demand is exhausted.

S/DD₁D₂D₃Supply
S₁(C:8)25 (C:3)(C:7)25
S₂20 (C:4)5 (C:6)10 (C:9)35
S₃(C:5)(C:7)30 (C:6)30

Total Cost = (25×3) + (20×4) + (5×6) + (10×9) + (30×6) = 455.

Degeneracy Check: 5 allocations = (3+3-1). Non-Degenerate.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Concept: Since W₂ cannot do J₃, we assign a very large cost 'M' to the cell (W₂, J₃) to prevent the algorithm from ever choosing it.
